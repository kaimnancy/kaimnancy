# **Literature Synthesis – Project Gauri**

Comprehensive evaluation of key foundational literature, policy documents, and empirical studies informing the platform design, behavioral frameworks, and strategic positioning for Project Gauri.

| Sno. | Paper Title | Literature / Content Summary | Why It Is Relevant to the Project | Citation |

| 1 | Digitization of Livestock in India (PIB Press Release) | Details India's NDDB- and DAHD-led digital livestock ecosystem: over 35.68 crore animals tagged with 12-digit "Pashu Aadhaar" ear tags under NDLM ("Bharat Pashudhan") for traceable vaccination, breeding, and treatment histories; over 17.3 lakh milk producers across 54 milk unions using AMCS (2.43 lakh farmers on the mobile Digital Passbook app); 198 milk unions and 15 federations using i-DIS; and GIS-based milk route optimization. | Establishes the national Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) foundation (NDLM, AMCS) that the project can align with or build atop, confirming institutional backing and growing digital readiness among Indian dairy farmers. | Press Information Bureau (PIB), Government of India. (Jan 2026). Digitization of Livestock in India. [PIB Press Release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2212724&reg=3&lang=1) |
| 2 | A study to identify the practices of the buffalo keepers which inadvertently lead to the spread of brucellosis in Delhi | Found 0% awareness of brucellosis and the low-cost IVRI S19 vaccine across all 11 districts of Delhi. Documented widespread high-risk habits—consuming raw milk, sleeping in sheds, not isolating sick cattle, skipping pre-purchase blood tests, handling abortions/placentas bare-handed (20–27% reported abortions/retained placenta), and not disinfecting sheds. | Validates severe grassroots knowledge gaps in preventive cattle care. Directly justifies building a localized Knowledge Base (vaccine schemes, early disease signs, daily Q\&A) paired with an agentic AI that maintains structured records, sends WhatsApp alerts, prompts missing details, and generates vet-ready reports. | Kant, N., Kulshreshtha, P., Singh, R., Mal, A., Dwivedi, A., Ahuja, R., ... & Bhatnagar, R. (2018). A study to identify the practices of the buffalo keepers which inadvertently lead to the spread of brucellosis in Delhi. BMC Veterinary Research, 14(1), 329\. |
| 3 | Factors influencing antibiotic prescribing behavior and understanding of antimicrobial resistance among veterinarians in Assam, India | Surveyed veterinarians (86% government-employed) and found that 98% had no written policy for veterinary health product use and 58% lacked on-site diagnostic facilities. Calls for stronger policy-to-field communication and clear prescribing guidelines for prudent antimicrobial use (AMU). | Highlights systemic bottlenecks on the veterinary side—specifically diagnostic gaps and lack of standardized protocols—reinforcing the value of AI-prepared animal health histories and structured pre-consultation reports to support overburdened field vets. | Eltholth, M., Govindaraj, G., Das, B., Shanabhoga, M. Frontiers in Veterinary Science, 9, 864813\. |
| 4 | Veterinary telemedicine: a new era for animal welfare | Evaluates remote consultations via video, phone, and chat as a low-cost, accessible complement to in-person veterinary care—effective for follow-ups, surgical site checks, and mobility assessments—while emphasizing the need for clearer regulatory oversight. | Directly validates integrating remote video consultations with nearby vets into the platform to eliminate travel delays for early diagnosis, enhanced by AI-generated pre-visit animal reports. | Abu-Seida, A. M., Abdulkarim, A., & Hassan, M. H. (2024). Veterinary telemedicine: a new era for animal welfare. Open Veterinary Journal, 14(4), 952\. |
| 5 | A review and future trends of precision livestock over dairy and beef cow cattle with artificial intelligence | Reviews AI and non-portable sensor systems (2D/3D video, computer vision, infrared thermography) for early physiological anomaly detection, locomotion tracking, Body Condition Score (BCS) monitoring, and oestrus prediction. Notes bovine heat cycles last 18–24 days (21-day average), puberty occurs at 9–15 months, and oestrus lasts only 12–18 hours. | Benchmarks SOTA AI in cattle care and supplies the exact reproductive timeline parameters needed to build automated breeding-cycle tracking and timely farmer alerts so narrow 12–18 hour heat windows are not missed. | Michelena, Á., Fontenla-Romero, Ó., & Luis Calvo-Rolle, J. (2025). A review and future trends of precision livestock over dairy and beef cow cattle with artificial intelligence. Logic Journal of the IGPL, 33(4), jzae111. |
| 6 | Decision support tools for agriculture: Towards effective design and delivery | Found 49% of farmers use decision support tools (software 28%, paper 22%, apps 10%). Adoption depends on tangible perceived benefits, accuracy, up-to-date information, and usability; peer-to-peer experience sharing among farmers emerged as the most effective dissemination channel. | Guides product UX and adoption strategy: the AI must deliver immediate, visible utility rather than abstract analytics, and rollout should leverage farmer groups and cooperatives for peer-led trust building. | Rose, D. C., Sutherland, W. J., Parker, C., Lobley, M., Winter, M., Morris, C., ... & Dicks, L. V. (2016). Decision support tools for agriculture: Towards effective design and delivery. | 7 | Debunking the myth of the hard-to-reach farmer: Effective communication on udder health | Demonstrates that "hard-to-reach" farmers are not homogeneous and fall into four behavioral segments based on trust in external sources and outward orientation: proactivists, do-it-yourselfers, wait-and-see-ers, and reclusive traditionalists. Tailoring channels and messaging—especially for do-it-yourselfers and wait-and-see-ers—significantly improves disease prevention. | Informs the AI's adaptive conversational design, allowing communication tone, framing, and outreach channels to be personalized to different farmer mindsets rather than relying on generic broadcasts. | Jansen, J., Steuten, C. D. M., Renes, R. J., Aarts, N., & Lam, T. J. G. M. (2010). Debunking the myth of the hard-to-reach farmer: Effective communication on udder health. Journal of Dairy Science, 93(3), 1296–1306. |
| 8 | Precision dairy farming: Opportunities and challenges for India | Surveys Indian precision dairy systems (INAPH, Chitale Dairy's cows-to-cloud and RFID/SMS Herdman platform, AFIMILK, Dairy Portal/DISK, BAIF's 18–19 day rapid pregnancy test) and identifies major barriers: small herd sizes (1–2 animals), high hardware/maintenance costs, weak rural IT infrastructure, and digital illiteracy. Notes strong ROI potential (e.g., preventing 1% of fraudulent claims can recover system costs). | Maps the competitive landscape in India and explains why hardware-heavy precision dairy tools fail smallholders—validating a low-cost, software- and messaging-first AI approach built for resource-constrained rural farmers. | Rathod, P., & Dixit, S. (2020). Precision dairy farming: Opportunities and challenges for India. Indian Journal of Animal Sciences, 90(8), 1083–1094. |
| 9 | A meta-analysis of fear appeals: Implications for effective public health campaigns | Synthesizes the Extended Parallel Process Model (EPPM), showing that threat-based messages drive productive action (danger control) only when high perceived threat (severity \+ susceptibility) is paired with high perceived efficacy (self-efficacy \+ response efficacy). Low efficacy triggers fear control (denial, avoidance, reactance). | Directly shapes the AI's "Reward \+ Concern \+ Next Efficient Step" messaging framework—pairing loss-framed financial risk alerts (e.g., potential milk loss) with immediate, zero-cost actions (e.g., one-tap vet card to a cooperative paravet) to prevent app muting or reactance. | Witte, K., & Allen, M. (2000). A meta-analysis of fear appeals: Implications for effective public health campaigns. Health Education & Behavior, 27(5), 591–615. |
| 10 | Using farmers' attitude and social pressures to design voluntary Bluetongue vaccination strategies | Uses the Reasoned Action Approach (RAA) to show that farmer attitude is the primary driver of voluntary vaccination, reinforced by injunctive and descriptive social norms. Recommends combining credible informational tools, financial incentives, and peer-group social pressure to maximize voluntary uptake. | Guides behavioral nudges and retention mechanics: combining trustworthy vernacular guidance, gain-framed routine reminders (21-day breeding/vaccine windows), and cooperative peer norms to boost voluntary preventive care. | Sok, J., Hogeveen, H., Elbers, A. R. W., & Oude Lansink, A. G. J. M. (2016). Using farmers' attitude and social pressures to design voluntary Bluetongue vaccination strategies. Preventive Veterinary Medicine, 133, 114–119. |
| 11 | Telemedicine for Livestock Health & Safety (NDLM Blueprint) | Quantifies India's livestock disease losses (FMD: \$3.16B, Brucellosis: \$3.4B, Cystic Echinococcosis: \$212.35M, Avian Influenza in Manipur: \$3.3M) and a \~41,000 shortage of veterinary institutions (65,894 available vs. 1.07 lakh needed for 53.58 crore livestock). Highlights rural tele-density opportunities and outlines AI roles in history-taking, visual identification, preliminary diagnosis, and decision support for Registered Veterinary Practitioners (RVPs). | Provides macro-level economic and policy justification for the project, quantifying the veterinary gap and explicitly validating human-in-the-loop AI and veterinary telemedicine within India's national livestock vision. | NITI Aayog. Telemedicine for Livestock Health & Safety (referencing the National Digital Livestock Mission Blueprint, October 7, 2021). |
